# Detailed Design


## Function of the Subsystem


The function of this subsystem is to prepare an existing golf car for the installation of a solar panel and a new battery system. Before any new components can be installed, the golf cart must be properly disassembled and cleared of existing parts that would obstruct or interfere with the upgrades. This subsystem serves as the foundational preparation phase within the overall system, ensuring that the vehicle is in the correct state for the electrical and mechanical upgrades that follow. 

The intended function of this subsystem, as aligned with the conceptual design, is to safely and systematically remove all necessary components from the golf cart so that when the installation starts the required areas of the vehicle without obstruction. Without this step being done correctly, this subsystem is responsible for the solar panel mounting and the battery installation and cannot be performed safely or effectively. This subsystem acts as the enabling step for the entire project. Its completion determines whether the rest of the system can proceed as designed. 


## Specifications and Constraints


- **29 CFR 1910.147 & CFR 1910 Subpart S/ NFPA 70E (OSHA Electrical Safety).**
    Requires lockout/tagout to be applied to the battery disconnect before disassembly begins, and minimum PPE safety glasses, acid- resistant gloves, and closed-toe footwear to be warn thought. Both measures protect team members from electrocution and arc-flash hazards during the removal process.

- **EPA and State Environmental Regulations.**
    Requires the lead-acid battery to be placed in secondary containment upon removal and transferred to a certified recycler with documented chain of custody. This applies directly to how the battery is handled and disposed of during disassembly, as improper management carries legal and environmental liability. 

- **Physics/ Structural Constraints.**
    The OEM roof panel must be removed without damaging the mounting rails, as they are the interface for another subsystem. The battery compartment must also be inspected and cleared of corrosion before handoff, as surface degradation would compromise the seating of the new LIFEPO4 unit. 

- **Socio-economic Constraints.**
    All removed components must be labeled and stored to avoid losing parts and exceeding budget. The initial vehicle condition report must be completed before any removal begins. 
    

## Overview of Proposed Solution


The proposed solution for this subsystem is a systematic, step-by-step disassembly procedure that prepares the golf car for the battery conversion and solar panel installation that follows. Before any physical work begins, the team will complete the initial vehicle condition report, capturing photos, and pre-modification baseline. This directly satisfies the documentation specifications.

The vehicle will be fully de-energized and a lockout/tagout procedure applied to the battery disconnect, bringing the work environment into compliance with the 29 CFR 1910.147. All team members will wear proper PPE before any components are touched. The existing lead-acid battery pack will then be removed, place into a secondary containment, and transferred to an EPA- compliant recycler. 

With the battery removed, the team will proceed to clear the remaining components that obstruct the installation areas. All removed parts will be labeled and stored within the project workspace to prevent loss and keep the project within budget. The OEM roof panel will be removed with care to preserve the integrity of the mounting rails, as these serve as the interface to the solar mounting kit in another subsystem. Lastly, the LiFePO4 will be installed and connected. Together, these steps ensure that every specification is met and every constraint is respected, leaving the vehicle fully prepared for the upgrades that follow.

## Analysis

 
**Golf Cart Prep Subsystem Analysis**

The Golf Cart Prep Subsystem serves as the critical foundation upon which all other subsystem depend. Without proper preparation of the golf cart existing structure, no subsequence installation can,  be performed safely, securely or effectively. This analysis demonstrates that the chosen preparation approach satisfies the core functional requirement: enabling the successful installation of all new components. 

**Roof Removal and Solar Panel Clearance**

Roof Removal and Solar Panel Clearance
The existing golf cart roof must be removed to create a clean, flat, and unobstructed mounting surface for the incoming solar panel. The stock roof on a standard golf cart is typically a lightweight plastic or fiberglass canopy that is not rated to support additional load such as a solar panel. By removing it and replacing it with a structurally appropriate surface, the subsystem ensures the solar panel has a stable, level platform to be mounted on. This step directly enables the Electrical Subsystem to proceed with solar panel installation and is therefore a non-negotiable prerequisite to overall system functionality.

**Battery Compartment Preparation**

Making room for the new battery involves clearing, repositioning, or removing existing components that occupy the intended battery space. This preparation ensures the battery can be seated securely, accessed for maintenance, and connected to the electrical system without interference or strain on cables and connectors.

Conclusion
The Golf Cart Prep Subsystem is straightforward in scope but essential in function. By removing the original roof to accommodate the solar panel and clearing space for the new battery system using non-permanent modification techniques, this subsystem creates the physical conditions necessary for every other subsystem to be installed correctly. The design is low-risk, reversible, and directly aligned with the project's installation requirements, making it both a practical and effective approach to system integration.
