## Function of the Subsystem

The function of this subsystem is to absorb sunlight and transfer it into energy to power the golf car while still acting as the roof of the golf car.

## Specifications and Constraints

**Bolting the solar panel frame to the four support posts**

The frame of the solar panel is not designed to fit a Club Car’s four vertical supports that hold up the roof. The spacing and location of the supports are fine, but the actual inserts that go into the back two supports are too wide and do not fit into the posts easily. Although it is possible to fit them into the supports, it is not recommended that this should be the standard.

**Feeding the wire to the battery**

There is no set or direct route for the wiring from the solar panel to the charge controller, so a new path will have to be made.


## Overview of Proposed Solution

The solution to the two back inserts on the solar panel frame that go into the back two supports of the golf car is that they need some adapter to snugly hold both the frame inserts and the golf car supports to ensure that it won’t cause unnecessary stress on the posts and break.

The solution for feeding the wiring from the solar panel to the battery will be that the wire will go from the back of the solar panel into the back post of the golf car and through the side wall of the battery compartment, as shown in Figure 1.1.

## Interface with Other Subsystems

**Inputs**

Solar energy supplied by the sun:

Golf car posts that hold the solar panel frame: The solar panel subsystem interacts with the frame of the car, specifically the four vertical supports that previously held the roof in place. Much like the previous roof, it is bolted down to the supports to keep the panel in place.

**Outputs**

Wire from panel to charge controller: The solar panel subsystem interacts mainly with the charging controller. This interaction is one-way, with the solar panel providing electricity to the battery through wires connected toward the back and under the solar panel.
