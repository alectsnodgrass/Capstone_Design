# Detailed Design: Battery Installation

# Introduction

The purpose of this detailed design is to define the aspects involved with the installation of a LiFePO4 battery for a golf cart. This information will help LaniEV create a conversation kit for golf carts manufactured by Club car. 

The proceeding sections in this document will discuss:
- the functions pertaining to the subsystem.
- specifications and constraints to are involved with the subsystem.
- an overview of the proposed solution.
- how this subsystem interacts with the other systems within this project
- the internal connections between the battery and each electrical circuit found in the cart.

## Function of the Subsystem

The battery installation serves as the focal point for every electric system that is part of the golf cart. These electric systems are, but not limited to: the engine, head lights, brake lights, and controller. With power being delivered to these systems, the golf cart will be able to operate as intended. 

Along with the electrical systems being able to operate, the installation serves to connect the solar panel to the battery. This allows the battery to be charged by either a wall outlet, through the use of an AC-DC converter, or by the solar cells inside the solar panel. After installation, the golf cart will have an eco-friendly charging system that does not require the grid. Although not perfect, this serves as the first step to finding a solution to powering golf carts without the need to be charged via the power grid.

While the battery installation is currently focused on the Club car brand, consideration into how the battery installation serves for E-Z-GO and Yamaha will also need to be looked into. This will be done over the summer. 


## Specifications and Constraints

The only specification required for the installation of the battery is proper documentation for LaniEV regarding the process and any adjustments made to the cart.

Below are possible constraints that may arise during the battery installation.

### Mounting Constraint
The cart was originally designed to houses two sets of three batteries. Each set used a battery clamp, a plastic plate which was pulled down using a metal rod, to hold each battery in place. Due to the conversion kit using one battery, it must be properly mounted in some manner so it does not move. If the battery were to move, it could lead to wires being pinched, or damage to the battery or internal frame depending on the force at which the battery moves.

### Electrical Constraint
During the installation of the battery, consideration of the required voltage will need to be kept in mind. If the correct battery is not chosen, it could deliver an incorrect voltage to the cart, some aspects such as the driving range or lifetime of the battery could occur [1]. 

### Circuit Constraint
Just like the electrical constraints, the wiring in the golf cart was meant to handle six batteries supplying a certain voltage to the cart. If the battery's discharge voltage is higher or lower than the recommended amount, the wiring connecting the multiple elements of the cart may be inadequate for this task. Thus, the original 16 gauge wiring inside the cart may need to be replaced with an appropriate wiring.

### Environmental Constraint
There are two major environmental factors which could damage the battery. The first is water damage. During heavy rainstorms, water could find a way underneath the front seats of the cart and make contact with the battery. If the water were to make contact with certain elements of the battery for an extended period of time, it could lead to external damages, for example the battery terminals, or internal damages if the water finds a way inside [2].

The second factor is lightning. Although it is not common for for a person or vehicle to be struck by lightning, approximately 1 in 3000 odds [3], it can still cause harm to the vehicle if lightning does strike. Depending on the magnitude of the discharged released by the bolt, the battery could be overloaded and fail, ranging from reduced lifespan to igniting or possibly exploding [4].

Each constraint listed above must be considered when working on the Club car, as well as the Yamaha and E-Z-GO in the future. 

## Overview of Proposed Solution

To combat these issues, it has been decided that a Lithium Iron Phosphate (LiFePO4) battery will be used. A LiFePO4 battery weight is lighter compared to a lead-acid battery, allowing for easier mounting and handling [5]. Because of the LiFePO4's light weight, it does not require much force to be kept in place, compared to a heavier loud which could create a greater force. 

The LiFePO4 can be bought to replace a 3.2V, 12V, 24V, and 48V system. Due to the Club car requiring a 48V battery supply, the LiFePO4 is able to complete this task. The LiFePO4 battery is able to discharge a steady voltage to the system over extended periods, meaning that power does not fluctuate if the battery is constantly on and in use [6]. In turn, this lowers the risk of the gauge wires inside of the cart from taking on larger currents, thus lowering the risk of failures appearing in the system. 

To ensure the battery is protected from possible water damage, an insulating material will be used to surround the battery. This material will be fitted so the mounting of the battery is not affected. With the material in place, water will not be able to damage the battery from the bottom if it were to get underneath the driver's seat. 

The LiFePO4 has built in safety mechanisms that allow it to protect against unwanted events [7]. One of the mechanisms is protection against overcharging. This protection comes from a Battery Management System (BMS) that is included inside the battery [8], which is used to monitor the internal parameters of the battery. If the battery were to receive an over flow of power, for our case a lightning strike, then the BMS will cut off the path the current is taking to reach the cells inside of the battery. The BMS also monitors the discharge rate, the charge of each cell to ensure balance, and the temperature of the battery.

## Interface with Other Subsystems

The LiFePO4's main purpose is to allow the cart to have a rechargeable battery using solar power, which requires a solar panel, and subsequent components, in order to capture and convert the sunlight into power. The Solar Energy Subsystem captures this need perfectly as it focuses on how the solar panel converts and delivers this voltage to recharge the battery. The battery does not deliver anything in return to the system, however a component called a charge controller monitors the battery [9]. Once the battery reaches maximum charge, the controller stops the flow of electricity between the solar panel and the battery. This prevents the solar panel from overcharging the battery and possible damaging it. 

The LiFePO4 battery interacts with the Solar Energy Subsystem through the frame of the golf cart. The Structural Frame Design helps ensure that there is enough space for wires to be safely passed from the top of the cart's rack to the interior where the battery is mounted. The frame acts to keep the wires connecting the solar panel and battery safe from outside elements, which can include environmental factors, animals, and humans. 

To ensure the battery is properly charged and is delivering power to each component throughout the cart, the Power Management and Charging system will be in constant communication with the battery. This system includes the charge controller, but also the gauge wires inside the system that help transfer power to and form the battery. The battery will communicate its internal charge, or the current voltage held in the battery, so that a device connected to the dashboard can tell the driver this information. The information about the battery read by the system will ensure that the battery does not become pressured by an increase in demand from the system, which can cause the quality of the battery to decrease.

## Analysis

For the cart to be fully capable of charging itself without the need of the power grid, the right battery must be chosen. This means the battery must be able to charge itself through some eco-friendly method, specifically solar. Thus, the LiFePO4 battery was chosen due to its ability to be charged through the use of solar energy, but can be charged through an AC outlet if needed. Since this battery will be used in conversion kits, it must handle different elements while also being easily installed by anyone. Thankfully, the LiFePO4 is lighter compared to lead-acid batteries and most lithium-ion batteries. This lighter weight comes from its compact size, allowing it to fit into the battery compartment of the golf cart. This smaller size also allows for a custom water proof material to be fitted and installed alongside the battery, allowing for additional protection in the event water finds a way into the compartment.

The LiFePO4 battery has a built in Battery Management System, or BMS, which monitors and turns off certain connections and systems so the battery stays at peak operating potential. This helps in the incident of a lightning bolt striking a golf cart. A strike would send an excess amount of voltage to battery, normal causing it to explode. However, the BMS turns off the receiving end of the battery, providing an extra layer of protection against overcharging the battery. Although it is still possible for the battery to be damaged, the BMS will help reduce overall damage compared to a lithium battery that does not have a BMS device. 

Although a consumer could choose other lithium-ion batteries to fit the specific needs for a golf cart, the LiFePO4 battery is the safest option. Its longer lifespan, light weight, eco-friendly charging method, and ability to output a steady voltage over long periods of time allow it to stand out amongst the competition.
