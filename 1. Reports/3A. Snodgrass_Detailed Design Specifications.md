# **Detailed Design: Structural Frame Design**

# 1. Introduction

## 1.1 Purpose

This document defines the detailed design of the **Structural Frame subsystem** for a universal solar-powered golf car conversion kit developed in collaboration with LaniEV. The subsystem provides the primary mechanical support structure for the solar energy system and associated electrical components.

# 2. Function of the Subsystem

The Structural Frame subsystem serves as the **primary mechanical support and integration platform** for the solar-powered conversion kit.

## 2.1 Primary Functions

- Supports and secures rigid roof-mounted solar panel
- Interfaces with OEM golf cart chassis (Club Car baseline platform)
- Provides structural mounting points for:
  - Solar panel
  - Electrical routing components
  - Optional charge controller enclosure
- Protects and routes electrical wiring through enclosed pathways
- Maintains structural integrity under:
  - Static loading (self-weight + solar panel)
  - Dynamic loading (vehicle motion, vibration, wind)

## 2.2 Compatibility Requirement

The subsystem is designed for **universal compatibility** across:
- Club Car (baseline test platform)
- E-Z-GO
- Yamaha

Compatibility is achieved through an **adjustable mounting architecture**.

# 8. Analysis (Structural)

The structural frame is modeled as a **beam-supported structure** subjected to distributed loading from the solar panel and frame components. The primary members of interest are the horizontal cross-members, which experience bending due to the applied load.


## 8.1 Load

The frame is modeled as a beam-supported structure subjected to:

- Distributed load from solar panel
- Frame self-weight
- Dynamic vehicle loading

Effective design load:

\[
W_{design} = \gamma_d (W_{panel} + W_{frame})
\]

Where:
- γ_d = 2.0 (dynamic amplification factor)

---

## 8.2 Bending Stress Analysis

The maximum bending stress in a beam is given by:

$ \sigma = \frac{M c}{I} $

### 8.2.1 Variable Definitions

- ($\sigma$) — Bending stress at the outer fiber $([\mathrm{Pa}]$)  
- ($M$) — Maximum bending moment $([\mathrm{N \cdot m}]$)  
- ($c$) — Distance from the neutral axis to the outermost fiber $([\mathrm{m}]$)  
- ($I$) — Area moment of inertia of the beam cross-section $([\mathrm{m}^4]$)

## 8.3 Determination of Bending Moment

For a simply supported beam with a uniformly distributed load:

$ M_{\max} = \frac{w L^2}{8} $

- ($w$) — Distributed load per unit length ($\mathrm{N/m}$)  
- ($L$) — Length of the beam between supports ($\mathrm{m}$)

### 8.3.1 Source

- Derived from classical beam theory (Euler–Bernoulli beam theory)  
- Reference: Mechanics of Materials textbooks (e.g., Hibbeler, Gere)

---

## 8.4 Deflection Analysis

The maximum deflection for a simply supported beam under uniform load is:

$ \delta_{\max} = \frac{5 w L^4}{384 E I} $

### 8.4.1 Variable Definitions

- ($\delta_{\max}$) — Maximum vertical deflection ($\mathrm{m}$)  
- ($w$) — Distributed load per unit length ($\mathrm{N/m}$)  
- ($L$) — Beam length ($\mathrm{m}$)  
- ($E$) — Young’s modulus of the material ($\mathrm{Pa}$)  
- ($I$) — Area moment of inertia ($\mathrm{m}^4$)

### 8.4.2 Source

- Derived from solving the Euler–Bernoulli beam differential equation:

   - $ E I \frac{\mathrm{d}^4 y}{\mathrm{d} x^4} = w  $

- Standard result for simply supported beams with uniform loading

---

## 8.5 Load Modeling Assumptions

- The solar panel weight is treated as a **uniformly distributed load** across the cross-members  
- Dynamic effects (vibration, acceleration) are approximated using a static equivalent load with a safety factor  
- Connections between members are assumed rigid for first-order analysis  

---

## 8.6 Design Criteria

The structural design must satisfy:

### 8.6.1 Strength Criterion

$ \sigma_{\max} < \frac{\sigma_{\text{yield}}}{\text{FoS}} $

- ($ \sigma_{\text{yield}} $) — Yield strength of the material  
- FoS — Factor of Safety (≥ 2.0)

---

### 8.6.2 Deflection Criterion

$ \delta_{\max} < \delta_{\text{allowable}} $

- ($ \delta_{\text{allowable}} $) — Maximum permissible deflection (set by design requirements)

Typical guideline:
- ($ \delta_{\text{allowable}} \approx \frac{L}{240} $) to ($ \frac{L}{360} $) for structural applications to prevent damage and maintain integrity

---

## 8.7 Material Considerations

For aluminum (typical structural alloy):

- \(E \approx 69 \times 10^9 \ \mathrm{Pa}\)  
- \(\sigma_{\text{yield}} \approx 150\text{–}300 \ \mathrm{MPa}\) (depends on alloy)

---

## 8.8 Thermal / Environmental Analysis

- Aluminum chosen for:
  - Corrosion resistance
  - High strength-to-weight ratio

- Drainage and sealing prevent:
  - Water accumulation
  - Electrical degradation

---

## 8.9 Summary

This analysis ensures that:

- The frame will not yield under expected loading conditions  
- Deflection remains small enough to:
  - Prevent panel damage  
  - Maintain structural integrity  
  - Avoid excessive vibration or fatigue issues  

Further refinement (e.g., Finite Element Analysis) can be used to validate stress concentrations and joint behavior.
